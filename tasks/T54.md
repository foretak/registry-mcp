## Part 0 — Recon. Write `tasks/T54-recon.md` first, in the style of `tasks/T48-recon.md`

Every fact `[fetched]`, `[portal]` or `[repo]`; numbers counted, not estimated; a verdict; the trap.
**Six things, and the first can end the task.**

1. **The terms of use, read in the portal and quoted verbatim.** What does the FCA permit a
   registered API user to do with the data — specifically, may it be relayed to a third party in a
   response? **If the portal's terms forbid relaying, stop and report: the task ends there and one
   paragraph in `DECISIONS.md` closes it**, exactly as D-045(b) closed VIES. Record the exact
   sentence that will become `SourceRef.license`, in D-038's and D-046(g)'s shape — the publisher's
   own words, and a stated absence if the publisher states nothing. **Do not reuse the Register
   Extract Service's licence language.** The RES handbook §2.12/§2.15 (*"You are not permitted to
   share or re-sell the data under this licence…"*) is a **different service** — §1.13 says so in
   terms — delivered under a paid subscriber agreement with a third-party processor. Quoting it here
   would be D-045(b)'s mistake in reverse. Say in the recon which document you read and that it was
   the API's own.
2. **The base URL, the version path and the auth headers.** DEPTH.md has `register.fca.org.uk/services/V0.1`
   with `X-Auth-Email` + `X-Auth-Key` marked `[search]`. **Confirm or correct it from the portal**,
   and record the header names exactly.
3. **The firm endpoints and their real response envelopes.** At minimum: the firm record by FRN, its
   permissions/activities, and its appointed-representative relationships. Record which of them
   exist, their exact paths, and what the envelope looks like — the FCA's Salesforce-backed services
   have historically wrapped results in a `Status` / `ResultInfo` / `Data` shape where a *not found*
   is a **200 with a status string**, not a 404. **Establish that specifically**, because a mapper
   that treats 200 as success will report an unauthorised firm as authorised.
4. **Rate limiting.** The published limit is **50 requests / 10 seconds**, primary-sourced (RES
   handbook §1.17), with §1.18: *"We do not offer any 'premium' versions of the API, nor the ability
   to raise the limits."* Record what the service actually sends on the wire — status code, headers,
   `Retry-After` — when you exceed it, and whether the limit is per key or per IP. One deliberate
   burst, then stop.
5. **The FRN↔CRN join.** Our lookup key for GB is a company registration number; the FCA's is a firm
   reference number. **Establish exactly how a caller gets from one to the other**, and be honest
   about what happens when they cannot. If the FCA's firm record carries a CRN, say where. If the
   only route is a name search, **that is a serious finding and it goes in the verdict**, because a
   name search is a fuzzy join and D-009 forbids guessing which of two firms is the one asked about.
   **Do not design around it in the recon; report it.**
6. **What of it is personal data.** Enumerate every field on every firm endpoint you will map, and
   name the ones that could carry a natural person's particular — a sole trader's firm name, an
   individual contact, a named complaints handler. The FCA's own privacy page confirms the register's
   services *"feature data about individuals carrying out specific roles"*, so the adjacency is real.
   This is D-042(e)'s sweep and it is the input to §B3.

contradicts anything in D-047(b) — the rate limit, the firms-only shape, the credential model —

## A. `core/registry.py` and `core/models.py` — the second credential

D-045(c)'s mechanism, built here for the first time:

```python
include_api_key_env: ClassVar[Mapping[str, str]] = {}   # include value -> env var name
```

on `Registry`, and published on `CountryInfo` as `include_api_key_env: dict[str, str]` (sorted keys,
for a stable wire), filled by `country_info()`. So `list_countries` answers *"the base lookup needs
`COMPANIES_HOUSE_API_KEY`; the `regulator` attachment needs `FCA_API_KEY` as well"* in one round trip
— D-017's whole purpose and D-042(d)(1)'s discoverability rule.

- **`requires_api_key` and `api_key_env` keep meaning the base lookup, unchanged**, because that is
  what every existing consumer reads them as. Do not widen either. Do not add a `requires_api_key`
  per include.
- **An attachment whose variable is unset raises `upstream_error` with a hint naming its own
  variable** — never the base one — and `lookup_with` then leaves the block absent with a `notes`
  sentence while **the lookup still succeeds** (D-042(b)). Test that the hint names `FCA_API_KEY` and
  not `COMPANIES_HOUSE_API_KEY`; a hint that names the wrong variable is the D-017 failure this
  mechanism exists to prevent.
- The check happens **at request time, not at import time** — a self-hoster who never asks for
  `regulator` must not be nagged about a key they do not need, and `list_countries` must keep working
  with neither key set.
- **The include is still declared when the key is absent.** `supported_includes` describes what the
  country's register offers, not what this deployment is configured for; `include_api_key_env` is how
  a caller learns the difference. An undeclared-because-unconfigured include would be two states in
  one field (D-011).

**A note for whoever builds HMRC VAT later, so the shape is not improvised then:** D-047(e)(2) rules
that a *two-secret* attachment (OAuth client id + secret) does **not** widen this mapping to a tuple —
the value names the id variable and the hint names both, which is D-037's answer reused. Nothing in
this task implements it; it is written here so that the next reader of `include_api_key_env` does not
invent a second mechanism.

## B. `core/models.py` — the block

### B1. Shape

Country-neutral names (D-042(g)); national vocabulary in **values** only. Insert after the GLEIF pair
and before `CompanyReport`; `CompanyReport.regulator: RegulatorBlock | None = None`, always present
per D-004, `null` on every lookup that did not ask.

```python
class RegulatorPermission(_Base):
    name: str | None = None          # the activity/permission, as the regulator words it
    status: str | None = None        # where the regulator publishes one, verbatim
    since: date | None = None

class RegulatorRelationship(_Base):
    role: str | None = None          # e.g. the register's own word for principal / appointed rep
    firm_reference: str | None = None
    name: str | None = None
    is_current: bool | None = None
    from_date: date | None = None
    to_date: date | None = None

class RegulatorBlock(_Base):
    regulator: str | None = None            # 'Financial Conduct Authority'
    firm_reference: str | None = None
    status: str | None = None               # the register's own authorisation status, verbatim
    status_effective_from: date | None = None
    permissions: list[RegulatorPermission] = []
    relationships: list[RegulatorRelationship] = []
    register_last_changed: date | None = None
    provenance: SourceRef
    notes: list[str] = []
```

Adjust field *names* only if Part 0 shows a name is wrong for the payload; **do not add fields** — a
field you want is a `DECISIONS.md` entry, not an implementer's call (D-042(g)). If Part 0 finds
something genuinely important that has no home here, **stop and report**.

### B2. Nullability, the D-011 contract

- **Absent block** — not asked for, the key is unset, or the fetch failed; a `notes` sentence on the
  report says which.
- **Present block with `firm_reference=None`** — the register was asked and **holds no record** for
  this company. That is a real and useful answer: *this company is not on the FCA register*, which
  for most British companies is the correct answer and is not the same as "we could not look".
- **Present block with a `firm_reference` and an empty `permissions` list** — the firm is on the
  register and the register publishes no permissions for it. **Never** the same as the two above.
  Say all three in the class docstring.

### B3. Firms only, and it has to be visible in the code

D-028(1) bars a person→companies index outright, and D-047(b) makes the rule the *block's* rather
than the FCA's — Finanstilsynet's Norwegian *virksomhetsregister*, the named future filler, covers
*"juridiske einingar (**personar eller selskap**)"* too.

- **No individual endpoint is called.** No approved persons, no controlled functions, no Directory of
  certified and assessed persons, no disciplinary records. `grep -rn "individual\|/CF\|Directory" src/registry_mcp/registries/gb/regulator.py`
  returning anything outside a comment explaining this paragraph is a defect, and it is a done-check.
- **`RegulatorRelationship` is firm↔firm only.** Both ends carry a firm reference number; an entry
  without one is dropped, with a `notes` sentence saying how many were dropped and why. A principal/
  appointed-representative pair is two companies, and it is modelled that way or not at all.
- **`RegulatorRelationship.name` and any firm name that could be a sole trader's is bound by
  D-028(1)** — never a lookup key, never an index, never searchable, never in a log (D-040) — and
  `legal/terms.md`'s list of the fields where a natural person's name can reach a response gains it.
  Part 0 §6 tells you whether it can; if it can, make the edit, and if it cannot, say so in the report
  and make none.

## C. `registries/gb/` — the client and the mapper

- **`regulator.py`** holds the mapper, pure and synchronous, mirroring `charges.py` / `insolvency.py`.
  **`client.py`** grows the fetch and the cache read/write. `__init__.py` adds `"regulator"` to
  `supported_includes`, the `include_api_key_env` entry, and the concrete `async def regulator(self, id: str) -> RegulatorBlock`.
- **A different host and a different credential from Companies House**, so a **separate `httpx`
  client, a separate limiter and a separate `SourceRef`** — never the Companies House client with a
  swapped header. The 50/10 s budget is the FCA's alone and does not touch the 600/5-min one.
- **The join.** Whatever Part 0 §5 finds, obey D-009: if the FRN cannot be derived from the CRN
  deterministically, the block is present with `firm_reference=None` and a `notes` sentence saying the
  register could not be joined on this identifier — **never a name-matched guess**, and never a list
  of candidates presented as an answer.
- **A 200 that means "not found"** (Part 0 §3) is mapped to the empty-record state, not to success.
  This is the trap of this task.
- **Rate limiting is enforced by us, not discovered from 429s.** 50 requests / 10 s, with the
  existing limiter shape; the recon's measured 429 behaviour is the fallback, not the plan.
- `notes` carries: the FRN used and how it was obtained; any dropped relationship rows and why; and
  the fact that the FCA register describes **regulated activity permissions**, not company status —
  a firm can be authorised and dissolved, or live and unauthorised, and this block says nothing about
  the second (`CompanyReport.status` does).

## D. `core/cache.py`

One row:

```python
"regulator": (24 * 60 * 60, 60 * 60),
```

**D-006's default pair, deliberately, and not `lei`'s 7 days.** An authorisation can be varied,
suspended or cancelled on a day, and a stale "authorised" is wrong in the direction that harms the
caller — the same argument D-042(j) made for charges. The empty TTL is **1 hour** for D-006's own
reason: a firm that has just been authorised must become visible fast. Put the reason in the comment.
Change nothing else in that file.

## Out of scope

- **Every individual endpoint**, permanently, by D-028(1) — not deferred, barred.

  is a guess.
- **Funds, collective investment schemes, sub-funds, waivers, exclusions, requirements and
  disciplinary history.** None of them is bought by the question this block answers.
- **A `is_authorised: bool` or any other derived verdict.** D-025(d) and D-011: an authorisation

  will not re-derive it.
