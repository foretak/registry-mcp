## Orchestrator addendum (2026-09-08, after T40) — ten facts the canonical descriptions lost, and one that never generalised

D-044(a) said every fact in the module docstrings was kept when the models moved to `core/models.py`. T40
diffed every field description before deleting the stand-ins and found these were **not** kept anywhere in
the tree. They are yours to restore, in the canonical field descriptions, in the same commit as the charge
work — your ownership of `core/models.py` is extended to **descriptions only** on `FiledDocument`,
`FilingHistory`, `InsolvencyEvent`, `InsolvencyCase`, `InsolvencyBlock` and `SourceRef.license`. Keep the
descriptions country-neutral in form (D-042(g)): a fact about one register is stated as that register's,
the way `days_from_fee_point` already does.

1. `FiledDocument.period_start` — Sweden's reason for `None`: deriving one by subtracting twelve months would assert a period length the register never stated; **bokföringslagen 3 kap. 3 § permits an 18-month first or final period**.
2. `FiledDocument.days_from_fee_point` — Sweden's datum: **årsredovisningslagen 8 kap. 6 § starts a förseningsavgift of 7 500 kr (15 000 kr for a public company)**; it is not the company's own deadline (**ÅRL 8 kap. 3 §**: within one month of the general meeting that adopts the accounts); and the **nine-month variant of 8 kap. 6 §** cannot be excluded because the dataset does not identify the companies it applies to.
3. `FiledDocument.category` — the 22 category words observed live in Britain, verbatim: `accounts`, `capital`, `officers`, `mortgage`, `confirmation-statement`, `annual-return`, `resolution`, `gazette`, `incorporation`, `address`, `insolvency`, `dissolution`, `change-of-name`, `persons-with-significant-control`, `auditors`, `miscellaneous`, `historical`, `restoration`, `document-replacement`, `change-of-constitution`, `return`, `other` (the list is not closed).
4. `FiledDocument.days_from_fee_point` (Britain) — the two profile fields that carry the *next* due dates, `accounts.next_accounts.due_on` and `confirmation_statement.next_due`, and that deriving a past datum would mean guessing a 9-month or 6-month period.
5. `FiledDocument.file_format` — Companies House's filing-history endpoint publishes only a page count and a `paper_filed` marker, no media type.
6. `InsolvencyEvent.event_type` — all thirteen words observed live in Britain, verbatim (read them back from `registries/gb/insolvency.py`'s docstring or the fixture; no lookup table backs this field, so the description is the only place the vocabulary is recorded).
7. `InsolvencyCase.note_codes` — the single code ever observed, `scottish-insolvency-info`, means the Accountant in Bankruptcy's Register of Insolvencies holds further detail this API does not.
8. `InsolvencyBlock.statuses` — all eight words observed live in Britain, verbatim (same reason as 6).
9. `FiledDocument.document_id` (Norway) — the payload also carries an integer `id`, an internal row identifier, which is not relayed.
10. `SourceRef.license` — for Norway's accounts block the value is NLOD 2.0, the same licence the company record on the same host carries, because the endpoint states no separate licence of its own; the generic description should say a block's licence is the register's own for that endpoint and may equal the base report's.
11. **A description that never generalised:** `FilingHistory.documents` says "newest first by `filed_at`" — that is Britain's sort key. Sweden sorts by `period_end` then `filed_at`; Norway by `period_end` then `period_start`. Say the order is newest first by the register's own period or filing date, and name the three keys.

Add nothing that is not in this list or in D-045(a). The drift test on the card's `outputSchema` will go red
for these as well — expected, reported, not regenerated.
