## The three regulations most likely to create demand for an agent-callable company lookup

**1. Norway's mandatory B2B e-invoicing — bokføringsloven § 10, from 1 January 2027.** Highest volume, nearest date, exact fit. Every bookkeeping-obliged Norwegian business (turnover ≥ NOK 50,000) must issue e-invoices to counterparties registered in ELMA, which means resolving `0192:<organisasjonsnummer>` before it can invoice. The lookup is per-counterparty and recurring, the identifier is the one the server already takes, and the register (Enhetsregisteret) is free and open under NLOD 2.0. Nothing else in this folder converts so directly into calls. *(File 06.)*

**2. The EU AMLR — Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, from 10 July 2027.** Widest reach and highest willingness to pay. Article 22(1)(b) enumerates the fields, Article 23(4) requires "a recently issued excerpt of the register" for every new legal-entity relationship, and Article 26(2) forces a refresh at least every 1 or 5 years depending on risk — across every obliged entity in 27 Member States on a single date. AMLA's draft RTS puts "the central register, business or company register" first in its list of acceptable sources. The catch: it demands *multi-country* coverage, which is the roadmap question. *(Files 01, 03.)*

**3. Norway's input-VAT rule — merverdiavgiftsloven § 8-1 with bokføringsforskriften § 5-1-2.** Not future demand — demand that exists today, with case law and a quantified penalty (NOK 492,541 reversed plus 40% surcharge in NS 27/2019). The identifier is on the invoice by statute, the check is continuous, the trigger is a missing three-letter suffix, and the register is already open. It is the shortest path from "here is the law" to "here is why you call this tool this week". *(File 09.)*

Runners-up, and why they rank lower: ViDA's core obligations are real but land in **2030** (file 04); the UK's ECCTA changes what the register *contains* rather than creating a duty to read it, though April 2028 adds small-company profit and loss (file 10); procurement duties are genuine and immediate but concentrated in a small number of contracting authorities (file 13).

## Key findings

**1. The AMLR turns a register lookup into a per-onboarding legal requirement in 27 countries on one date.** Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 Article 90: "It shall apply from **10 July 2027**". Article 22(1)(b) lists what must be obtained for a legal entity — legal form, name, registered-office address, country of creation, legal representatives, "**the registration number**, the tax identification number and the Legal Entity Identifier". Article 23(4) requires, for every new business relationship with a legal entity, "**valid proof of registration or a recently issued excerpt of the register confirming validity of registration**". Article 26(2) caps the refresh interval at **1 year** for higher-risk customers and **5 years** for everyone else. Article 24(1) requires BO discrepancies to be reported to the central register "**within 14 calendar days**". ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1624/oj/eng))

**2. Norway has already legislated the clearest single lookup obligation in Europe.** Lov 19. juni 2026 nr. 39 inserts into bokføringsloven § 10: "Dokumentasjon for salg av varer og tjenester til andre bokføringspliktige skal utstedes i **elektronisk fakturaformat**", commenced from **1 January 2027** (receiving and digital bookkeeping from 1 January 2030). Prop. 44 L (2025–2026) scopes the duty to counterparties "**registrert i Elektronisk mottakerregister (ELMA)**", and DFØ's guidance says the lookup key is "landkoden, f.eks. Norge **0192:** + organisasjonsnummer". Exemptions: turnover under **NOK 50,000**, certain bankrupt estates, finance/insurance/pension undertakings. ([regjeringen.no](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/nye-lovregler-om-e-fakturering-i-naringslivet-og-enkelte-andre-lovendringer-pa-finansmarkedsomradet-settes-i-kraft/id3166726/), [Lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/LTI/lov/2026-06-19-39), [anskaffelser.no](https://www.anskaffelser.no/verktoy/veiledere/mottakere-av-ehf-og-peppol-bis))

**3. The Norwegian VAT rule is a live, litigated, penalty-backed reason to check an organisasjonsnummer today.** Bokføringsforskriften § 5-1-2 requires the sales document to state "selgers navn og **organisasjonsnummer** … Dersom selger er registrert i Merverdiavgiftsregisteret, skal organisasjonsnummer etterfølges av bokstavene **MVA**". Skatteklagenemnda NS 27/2019 (20 March 2019) reversed **NOK 492,541** of input VAT and added a 20% ordinary plus 20% enhanced surcharge (NOK 98,508 each), holding: "Skattepliktige skulle selv ha kontrollert hvorfor selger utsteder fakturaer inkludert merverdiavgift når det ikke står MVA bak org nr." NS 116/2018 shows the duty is **continuing**, not a one-off check at onboarding. ([Lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2004-12-01-1558/KAPITTEL_5-1), [Skatteetaten](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/rettskilder/type/vedtak/skatteklagenemnda/etterberegning-av-inngaende-merverdiavgift-nar-selger-ikke-er-registrert-i-merverdiavgiftsregisteret.-skjerpet-tilleggsskatt/))

**4. A regulator has already told banks to check the company register in automated onboarding.** EBA/GL/2022/15, applying from **2 October 2023**, paragraph 38: remote onboarding solutions must allow verification that "where the customer is a legal entity, **it is publicly registered, where applicable**". Paragraph 21 extends the guidelines to "fully automated remote customer onboarding solutions … without or with little human intervention", and paragraph 9(c) requires firms to document "which steps are fully autonomized and which steps require human intervention". ([EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library/Publications/Guidelines/2022/EBA-GL-2022-15%20GL%20on%20remote%20customer%20onboarding/1043884/Guidelines%20on%20the%20use%20of%20Remote%20Customer%20Onboarding%20Solutions.pdf))

**5. BO data is gated everywhere, and Norway's gate is narrower than the EU's.** After CJEU C-37/20 & C-601/20 (22 Nov 2022) struck down general public access, AMLD6 Article 12(2) restored access for listed categories including "**(j) providers of AML/CFT products**, to the strict extent that products … are provided only to customers that are obliged entities or competent authorities". Norway's own Act (LOV-2019-03-01-2 § 11) lists only obliged entities, public authorities, media, civil-society organisations and higher-education institutions — **no product-provider category**. brreg grants API access only after an emailed application to opendata@brreg.no citing a legal basis, and only by lookup on organisasjonsnummer. ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62020CJ0037), [Lovdata](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2019-03-01-2), [brreg](https://www.brreg.no/bruke-data-fra-bronnoysundregistrene/datasett-og-api/data-om-reelle-rettighetshavere/))

**6. VIES answers one question and refuses several others.** A live call on 2026-09-05 to `POST /rest-api/check-vat-number` returned `valid: true`, `name: "GOOGLE IRELAND LIMITED"` and an address for IE — but the `requestIdentifier` (the proof-of-consultation reference) comes back empty unless the caller supplies its own VAT number, several Member States return `"---"` for name and address, and `GET /rest-api/check-status` listed **28** participants (27 Member States plus **XI**, Northern Ireland — Great Britain is out). ([Commission VIES](https://ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies/))

## What this means for registry-mcp

The demand is real, dated and mostly outside the project's control — which is the good kind of demand. Three consequences.

**Norway 2027 is the deadline to build for.** From 1 January 2027 a Norwegian bookkeeping-obliged business cannot lawfully issue a B2B sales document without resolving the counterparty's organisasjonsnummer and, in practice, checking ELMA. That is the same identifier `lookup_company` and `validate_company_id` already take. An ELMA/Peppol capability check alongside the register answer would make the tool complete for the single largest scheduled increase in Norwegian lookup volume — and it is a public directory, unlike beneficial ownership.

**Sell the provenance, not the answer.** Every instrument in this folder — AMLR Art. 20(4) and 21(3), AMLA's draft RTS Article 8, EBA/GL/2022/15 paras. 22 and 38 — asks for a named official source, its currency, and a record of what was consulted. `source`, `source_url`, `license`, `fetched_at`, `cached` and the rule that `null` means "this register does not say" are the compliance-relevant features. The `applies_because` string on deadlines is the same idea. Keep them; make them the pitch.

**Do not promise beneficial ownership.** Norwegian BO data is API-only, application-only, and the statutory access list has no category for a product vendor. The EU will have one from 10 July 2026 (AMLD6 Art. 12(2)(j)) but only for customers who are obliged entities or authorities. A hosted open BO endpoint is not available on current law; saying so plainly is more credible than hedging.

One correction to make while the deadline code is fresh: file 12 notes that the weekend/holiday roll-forward rule is written into a-opplysningsforskriften § 2-1 but **not** into skatteforvaltningsforskriften § 8-3-10 or regnskapsloven § 8-3. `applies_because` should not cite a section for a rule that section does not contain.

## Open questions we could not answer

1. **What does "recently issued" mean in AMLR Article 23(4)?** No definition in the Regulation; AMLA's draft RTS does not fix a maximum age either. Whether a 24-hour cache satisfies it is undecided, and it is the single most commercially important unknown here.
2. **Will AMLD6 Article 12(2)(j) be transposed anywhere in a way a small vendor can actually use?** Member States had until 10 July 2026. No transposition text was examined. The Article 11(5) notifications due 10 October 2026 will be the first comparable picture.
3. **Will Norway extend BO access beyond the § 11 list?** The Act empowers the Ministry to do so by regulation (§ 11(2)–(3)); no such regulation was found. If Norway aligns with AMLD6's Art. 12(2) categories, the answer to "can we offer Norwegian BO?" changes.
4. **Norway's samordnet aksjeeierregister.** The pre-project was due to the Ministry of Finance by end-2025. No proposal, no funding decision and no launch date located as of 2026-09.
5. **Danish primary sources.** erhvervsstyrelsen.dk returns HTTP 403 to automated requests and retsinformation.dk is a client-rendered SPA. The 1 July 2024 and 1 January 2025 bogføringsloven phases, the 7-day registration rule and the exact wording of §§ 15–16 could not be read in the original.
6. **French and Polish statutes.** Legifrance and podatki.gov.pl both refused automated fetching. The French calendar is cited from the Commission's eInvoicing France factsheet; the attribution to article 91 of loi n° 2023-1322 is unverified. Polish transitional dates for micro-enterprises come from secondary tax-practice sources.
7. **No regulator statement on *agentic* AI in KYC was found.** Only vendor and consultancy commentary. FATF's 2021 new-technologies report, the EBA's 2020 opinion on innovative CDD solutions and the Bank of England/FCA AI surveys were identified but not read in the original; they are the obvious next primary sources.
8. **Whether any Member State will exercise the ViDA Article 168 option** (input-VAT deduction conditional on holding a compliant e-invoice). That option, if widely taken, converts supplier validation from prudence into cash flow across the EU — but it is a national choice not yet made.
9. **The debarment list's real volume.** The UK list is published as a PDF, not an API, and appears to hold very few entries. Whether s.28 sub-contractor screening generates meaningful lookup demand in practice is unmeasured.
10. **The content of "core supplier information"** under the UK Procurement Regulations 2024 — reg. 6 requires it to be current at award, but the defining provision was not read in the original.
