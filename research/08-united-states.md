# 08 — United States

**Scope:** whether and how registry-mcp could serve United States company data — the fifty-one state registers, the federal layer that is not a register, the compliance deadlines, the vendors and MCP servers already there, and a verdict.
**Compiled:** 2026-09-05, extending `02-registers-landscape.md`'s United States entry and closing its open question 8 ("Which US states publish free bulk or API access? Only New York was checked").
**Full folder:** `~/research/registry-mcp/08-united-states/` — five files plus a README.
**Method note:** the session's WebSearch budget was **already exhausted (200/200)** when this work began, and archive.org was offline throughout. Every fact below comes from a **direct fetch or a live API call**, and roughly half the United States is recorded as **"not checked"** rather than guessed at.

## Key findings

**1. Seven states — not one — publish a statewide business register as free, keyless, machine-readable data.** Verified live 2026-09-05 via Socrata `/api/views/<id>.json` and `$select=count(*)`: **Colorado** 3,105,488 rows (`data.colorado.gov/4ykn-tg5h`), **Texas** 3,444,358 (`data.texas.gov/9cir-efmm`), **Pennsylvania** 2,360,829 distinct (`data.pa.gov/3urc-uaba`), **Oregon** 1,575,125 (`data.oregon.gov/tckn-sxa6`), **Connecticut** 1,295,905 (`data.ct.gov/n7gp-d28j`), **Virginia** 465,855 corporations (`data.virginia.gov` CKAN `datastore_search`), **New York** 4,275,591 (`data.ny.gov/n9v6-gdp6`). `13-united-states-edgar-states-gleif.md` treated New York as the exception; it is not. (`01-fifty-state-access-survey.md` §1, §3)

**2. Connecticut publishes a per-company annual-report due date — the second register in the whole library to do so, after Companies House.** `annual_report_due_date` populated for **587,920** of 1,295,905 rows, plus a `sub_status` carrying **`Annual report past due` (68,100)** and **`Admin Dissolution Initiated` (22,779)**. Daily, `license: "Public Domain"`, keyless. Richer than Companies House, which publishes the date but not the administrative consequence — and the second independent chance, after Ireland, to prove `published_deadlines` generalises beyond the UK. (`01-…` §3, `03-…` §4)

**3. Colorado publishes good standing as a status value, and most Colorado entities are not in it.** `entitystatus` counted live: **`Delinquent` 1,144,723**, **`Good Standing` 1,022,462**, `Voluntarily Dissolved` 596,033, `Administratively Dissolved` 157,836, and eleven more — fifteen self-describing English values, no code table needed. (`01-…` §3)

**4. Texas gives away the enforcement outcome for 3.4 m entities and charges a dollar for a search.** The Secretary of State: *"There is a $1.00 statutorily authorized fee associated with each search…"* The Comptroller, free and keyless, publishes `secretary_of_state_sos_or_coa_file_number`, `sos_status_code` and **`right_to_transact_business_code`** (`A` 3,248,888 · `N` 130,146 · `U` 45,213 · `D` 19,815) — and names *"Form 05-211, Texas Notice of Intent to Forfeit Right to Transact Business"* on the same page as the deadline: *"The annual franchise tax report is due May 15. If May 15 falls on a weekend or holiday, the due date will be the next business day."* **That is the roll-forward rule written into the same sentence as the date** — the thing Norway's statutes do not do (`AGENT_PRIMER.md` §8). Watch the whitespace: `"A "`, `"A\n"` and `"  "` are separate values in that column. (`01-…` §2, `03-…` §3)

**5. About half the United States refuses a scripted HTTP client at the front door.** 51 SOS business-search URLs probed live: **27 answered, 13 returned 403, 9 did not connect.** California is the sharpest: a JSON POST to `bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov/api/Records/businesssearch` returned **HTTP 429** with body `"description":"This request was blocked by our security service"`. No terms clause was needed. Florida, Ohio, Illinois, Georgia and Pennsylvania's filing portal all refused an automated fetch, so their bulk/API status is **not checked, not "none"**. (`01-…` §2, §5)

**6. Not one state publishes a licence instrument.** Colorado and Connecticut pick Socrata's `PUBLIC_DOMAIN`; Pennsylvania picks `USGOV_WORKS` (arguably the wrong label — 17 U.S.C. § 105 removes copyright from *federal*, not state, works). **Texas, New York and Oregon publish `license: null`**; Virginia publishes CKAN's `"notspecified"`. **Four of the seven open registers say nothing at all.** A dropdown value is evidence of intent, not a licence instrument. (`01-…` §1, `05-…` §6)

**7. Two freshness traps, one of them in the existing library file.** New York's flagship dataset is a **monthly snapshot** by its own metadata — *"Posting Frequency": "Monthly"*, *"Data is accurate as of the last business day of the month and year specified in the dataset name"* — so its `rowsUpdatedAt` of today means the file was *pushed* today, not that the facts are. `13-…` reads it as live. Virginia is worse: its Corporation file was last modified **2026-03-24**, five and a half months before the fetch. Same failure mode as OffeneRegister.de, smaller magnitude. (`01-…` §2, §3)

**8. The federal beneficial-ownership register was abolished for American companies in 2026, and the rest of the federal layer is unusable.** FinCEN, alert dated 11 August 2026: *"U.S. companies are exempt from the Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reporting requirements and therefore, are no longer required to file BOI reports."* The March 2025 interim final rule was made permanent and *"The final rule became effective on August 14, 2026."* Only certain foreign-formed companies still report; BOI access was never public. Alongside it: SAM.gov's open tier is *"10 requests/day"* by GSA's own table (1,000 with a SAM.gov role) and covers federal awardees, not companies; the IRS publishes nonprofits and nothing else; the EIN is not public. **The AMLR-shaped tailwind driving the European roadmap has an American headwind.** (`02-federal-layer.md` §1–§3)

**9. One EDGAR MCP server outweighs the entire company-registry MCP field, and there is no MCP server for any US state register.** `stefanoamorelli/sec-edgar-mcp`: **355 stars**, **3,960 PyPI downloads last month**, licence **AGPL-3.0** — against ~1,900/month for every company-registry MCP server combined (`AGENT_PRIMER.md` §3) and 236 for the leading brreg server. Twenty-plus EDGAR clones exist; exactly one has more than 2 stars — the brreg pattern at American scale. Searches for `company registry mcp secretary of state`, `mcp sunbiz`, `mcp business entity search state` and `kyb mcp server` each returned **zero**. (`04-aggregators-vendors-and-mcp-competitors.md` §1, §2)

**10. Both "just reuse the existing thing" shortcuts are closed by licence.** OpenCorporates is no longer keyless — `GET api.opencorporates.com/v0.4/companies/us_de/…` → **HTTP 401**, docs confirm *"An API key is required…"* — and its ODbL share-alike is unchanged. `sec-edgar-mcp` is AGPL-3.0. Neither can sit inside an MIT hosted API. (`04-…` §2, §3)

**11. No US identifier examined has a check digit.** Nine formats across seven states: CT 7 digits (*and* an 18-char Salesforce `id` — two candidate identifiers), TX 11-digit taxpayer number *and* 10-digit SOS file number, PA 10 digits zero-padded, VA 8 digits published **tab-prefixed and space-padded** (`"\t11763213  "`). None has a checksum. (`01-…` §6)

## What this means for registry-mcp

**D-027 fits without amendment.** `country: "US"` + `subdivision: "US-CT"` + `register_office: "Connecticut Secretary of the State, Business Services Division"` expresses every state cleanly, and each dataset's `attribution` field supplies `register_office` verbatim. Three US wrinkles need `notes`, not model changes: **`register_office` is sometimes not the register** (Texas's best free data is the Comptroller's); **`subdivision` names the register, not where the business is** — Delaware's own page says *"More than 66% of the Fortune 500 have chosen Delaware as their legal home"*, so a Delaware LLC trading in California is `US-DE` and that is the common case; and **one company is often registered in several states at once**, domestic in one and "foreign" in the others, so a US report answers "what does *this* state say", never "where is this company registered" (D-026(c)).

**`validate_company_id` must return `checksum_valid: null` for every US state, never `true` or `false`.** With no check digits anywhere it can normalise (stripping is mandatory — Virginia ships a leading tab, Texas ships `"A\n"` as a status value) and check length and character class, and nothing more. That is D-011's lesson applied before the mistake. Be honest too that the tool's Norwegian value proposition — reject a bad id before spending a real lookup — degrades to cosmetic here.

**The verdict: do not, with one triggered exception.** The country order in `02-registers-landscape.md` — Sweden, Finland, Ireland, France, Switzerland, Estonia — stands untouched. Each is one national register, with a licence in writing, serving a buyer that European regulation is about to compel (Norwegian e-invoicing 1 Jan 2027; AMLR 10 July 2027). The US has none of the three, and in 2026 moved the other way.

**The exception, on a trigger rather than a schedule:** if an identified user asks for a US state by name, **`US-CT` and `US-CO` are a cheap, unusually good pilot** — one keyless GET each, daily refresh, the only two large states that name a licence, and Connecticut proves `published_deadlines` generalises. Three preconditions: read both states' terms pages (**nobody has read any of the 51**); **omit Connecticut's `business_email_address` and its five ownership-demographic booleans** — they are not registration facts and D-026(b)'s reasoning about *passing on* applies; and ship the `checksum_valid: null` note first.

**Do not** build a `US` module claiming national coverage (two states is two states), an EDGAR-only `US-SEC` module (it answers a different question, would make `not_found` a lie for 99.9 % of American companies, and enters a saturated field as the twenty-third wrapper), anything routing around California's WAF, or anything depending on OpenCorporates or `sec-edgar-mcp`.

**One free thing, today:** when D-027 is implemented, make the US the worked example in the field descriptions — `"US-DE"` / `"Delaware Division of Corporations"` teaches that `subdivision` is where the *register* is, not the business, better than any German example will.

## Open questions

1. **Does Florida publish free bulk corporate data over SFTP?** `dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/other-services/data-downloads/` served a Cloudflare challenge to every automated fetch on 2026-09-05 and archive.org was offline. If true, the third-largest state joins the open list and the shortlist changes shape. **One browser visit answers it** — highest value per minute in the folder.
2. **Do Colorado's and Connecticut's terms of use permit redistribution through a hosted API?** Not one of the 51 states' terms pages was read. A `PUBLIC_DOMAIN` dropdown value is evidence of intent, not a licence instrument. The entire "do later" plan is blocked on two web pages.
3. **Is there any American demand for *registration* data via MCP, as opposed to *disclosure* data?** Evidence is one-sided and negative: 3,960 downloads/month for EDGAR, zero state-register MCP servers, four funded KYB vendors (Cobalt, Middesk, Enigma, Baselayer) serving the paid market, and no regulatory driver. One real user request would outweigh any further desk research.
4. **Every statute section behind every US deadline.** No DGCL, Texas Tax Code, California Corporations Code, Connecticut General Statutes or Colorado Revised Statutes section was read — every rule in `03-…` is quoted from an *agency page*. **A US `applies_because` cannot cite a section on this folder's authority.** Related: does *Feist* leave state register compilations uncopyrightable? That is the whole legal basis for treating `license: null` as low-risk in the US, and it was cited from general knowledge, not read.
5. **Whether any of the seven open datasets is the register of record or a derived extract.** Texas's is explicitly the franchise-tax roll; Pennsylvania's is a filtered view; New York's is a monthly snapshot. None was reconciled against its state's own web search for the same company. Ohio's and Delaware's bulk products are likewise unchecked — Ohio's site was in maintenance all session, Delaware's stats page is a 404.
