# 09 — China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan

**Scope:** whether registry-mcp can serve company data for the Chinese-speaking world, and on what terms.
**Compiled:** 2026-09-05, from statutes read on 中国人大网, two ministry/standards publications read in full, and **live HTTP probes of every register and vendor named** — twelve successful unauthenticated calls to Taiwan's register, reproducible refusals from three Chinese hosts.
**Full folder:** `~/research/registry-mcp/09-china-hong-kong-taiwan/` (6 files + README, every fact with URL, fetch date and confidence).
**Constraint:** the session's 200-call WebSearch budget was exhausted before this folder began. Everything was reached by direct URL fetch; where that was impossible — press reporting, most Western vendors' pricing — the gap is recorded as a gap.

---

## Key findings

**1. Taiwan is a shippable module, and the only one in the region.** From a Norwegian IP, no key, no account, no form:
`data.gcis.nat.gov.tw/od/data/api/5F64D864-61CB-4D0D-8AD9-492047CC1EA6?$format=json&$filter=Business_Accounting_NO eq 22099131`
returned TSMC with registered capital `280500000000`, paid-in capital `259323700670`, chairman, address, registering authority and two dates. Eleven further calls — directors, business scope, keyword search, entity-type check, **daily dissolution feed** — all returned data. No rate limit hit. That endpoint has served **21,745,118 calls** since 2013 (the platform publishes the counter), so it is load-bearing for Taiwanese software and unlikely to be withdrawn.

**2. Its licence is the cleanest in the folder.** Open Government Data License v1 grants use *"不限目的、時間及地域、非專屬、不可撤回、免授權金"* (any purpose, no limit of time or territory, non-exclusive, irrevocable, royalty-free), permits sub-licensing outright, and is *"與『創用CC授權 姓名標示 4.0 國際版本』相容"* — **CC BY 4.0 compatible**. Attribution is the sole obligation, and failing it *"視為自始未取得開放資料之授權"*. Compatible with an MIT project's hosted API in a way OpenCorporates' ODbL is not.

**3. Its identifier algorithm is published by the ministry that issues it, and changed on a date.** MOF Fiscal Information Agency, 2021-12-22: *"檢查邏輯由可被『10』整除改為可被『5』整除"* — the 統一編號 check rule went from divisible-by-10 to divisible-by-5; systems had to be amended by **31 March 2023**; new numbers issue from **April 2023**. The attachment gives the weights `1 2 1 2 1 2 4 1`, the digit-sum method, the 7th-digit-is-`7` special case, and five worked examples — including **`04595252`, valid under the new rule and invalid under the old**. Every check-digit rule in folder 02 is marked `VERIFY`; this one is not.

**4. Mainland China's register is not machine-reachable.** `gsxt.gov.cn` and its Sichuan and Guangdong mirrors returned **HTTP 521** behind a JavaScript cookie challenge; `samr.gov.cn` and `english.samr.gov.cn` returned **403**. No API, no bulk data, no developer portal, no licence statement of any kind — a harder wall than Germany's.

**5. Two of the four Chinese data majors refuse Norwegian traffic, in writing.** 天眼查 → **419**: *"根据相关法律规定，当前所在地区暂不支持访问 / According to relevant legal regulations, access is temporarily not supported in your current location"*, naming our IP and *"Current location: NO"*. 启信宝 → **451 Unavailable For Legal Reasons**, body naming the caller's city as `奥斯陆` (Oslo). 企查查 and 爱企查 returned 200. The vendors' own words beat the press reporting this pass could not cite.

**6. PIPL reaches us and has no legitimate-interests limb.** Art. 3 extends the law to processing outside the PRC that *"分析、评估境内自然人的行为"* — which is what a due-diligence lookup on a 法定代表人 does. The only workable basis is Art. 13(6), processing *"在合理的范围内"* information already lawfully disclosed. **That "reasonable scope" is exactly what D-028(1)'s company→officers-only rule preserves and a person→companies index would destroy** — the strongest external corroboration of D-028 in the library. Art. 39 also demands *separate consent* for cross-border transfer, unobtainable at register scale; that burden sits on the Chinese exporter, which is why finding 5 happens.

**7. Qichacha publishes per-call prices, and there is no margin in them.** 企业工商信息 **0.20 元/次** · 模糊搜索 **0.10** · 企业信息核验 **1.00** · full KYB bundle including 受益所有人 and 实际控制人 **3.00 元/次**. Volume packages are prepayment, not discount. Against folder 01's 16–20p Western wholesale, a *richer* payload sells at or below the floor. The gate is not price: 「限企业实名用户使用」 (corporate real-name verification) plus use-case review. Whether a Norwegian ENK can pass it is unknown — folder 02's eligibility question again.

**8. Hong Kong's register is excellent and has no search API.** ICRIS was rebuilt and merged with e-Registry into the e-Services Portal *"on 27 December 2023"* (`icris.cr.gov.hk` now 302-redirects, verified live). The only system-to-system interface, TPSI, is defined by the Registry as one *"to support software developers/suppliers to transmit/upload data"* — **filing only**. Fees verbatim: company particulars report **HK$22**; director index search **HK$11**/**HK$22**; director particulars **HK$11**; e-Search subscription **HK$500** + **HK$100** per extra account. Name searches free — the Endole shape, run by a government.

**9. HK closed director addresses in three dated phases** (Companies (Amendment) (No. 2) Ordinance 2018): **23 Aug 2021**, **24 Oct 2022**, **27 Dec 2023** — the last on the same day as the ICRIS revamp. Systemic replacement by a correspondence address and individual withholding on application are **two states, neither of them "absent"** — precisely what `suppressed: bool | None` carries, and cleaner than Companies House, where D-028 records that we still do not know whether suppression is signalled or silent.

**10. The MCP field here is crowded and hollow.** Qichacha ships its own MCP («让 AI Agent 读懂中国企业») with third-party OAuth connectors built around it — the Proff Premium situation repeated: the incumbent got there first, keyed and paid. The two GitHub servers claiming *"official GSXT/SAMR registry data"* were each **created and last pushed on a single day**, at 1 and 0 stars — and cannot be doing what they claim, since gsxt answers 521. The one measurable server does **93 npm downloads a month**.

**11. The USCC carries its own sub-national key.** Chars 3–8 of the 18-character 统一社会信用代码 are the GB/T 2260 administrative-division code (`310000` Shanghai); chars 1–2 give authority and category (`91` = SAMR-registered enterprise). A **validate-only** module can tell an agent *where in China* an entity is registered, with no lookup and no data access. GB 32100-2015 is confirmed mandatory and in force (2015-10-01) **with an Amendment No. 1** — but its text is served as page images to a JS-only viewer and **could not be read**.

## What this means for registry-mcp

**Ship `TW`.** On folder 02's rubric: **Access 5 · Market 2 · English 1 · Publishes 5 = 13** — losing everything on English, nothing on risk. First non-European country, the proof point "one shape, any country" has not yet earned, and it gives a Nordic importer a *richer* record than the UK register gives them, free. Send the 使用告知書 to `opendata.gcis@gmail.com` with the hosted egress IP first: the platform documents a source-IP whitelist that did not bite in twelve calls, and one email removes the module's only structural uncertainty.

**Then `CN` validate-only**, once GB 32100-2015 is read: USCC check character plus division decoding, with `lookup_company` returning `NOT_IMPLEMENTED` (501) and D-007's hint naming gsxt.gov.cn — the exact pattern D-028(6) chose for Norwegian beneficial owners, which proves it generalises. Two rules for the tool text: **"format valid" must never read as "this company exists"**, and a 15-digit input (the pre-2015 工商注册号, still on old contracts) must be named, not rejected. Do not ship the algorithm on this folder's authority; Sweden's still-unsourced Luhn claim is the precedent for what happens when that slides. **One new DECISIONS question falls out:** how discovery announces a country that answers one tool and not the others — `CountryInfo` has room, same shape as D-027(d)'s `subdivisions`, cheaper to rule now.

**Do not build `HK` or `CN` lookups.** HK: no search API, per-report pricing against a product whose thesis is that per-lookup pricing is a lost war, and an unanswered redistribution question — folder 02's Netherlands objection, equally dispositive. Mainland: unreachable register, no licence, PIPL exposure, and a supply chain where two of four vendors already refuse us on a stated legal basis and could refuse the rest with a 451 and no notice. Macau is out on absence of demand.

**Four traps for any TW spec.** **ROC dates** — `0760221` is 民國 76 = **1987-02-21**; parsed as Gregorian every company is founded a century early, silently. **HTTP 200 for everything** — a non-existent UBN returns 200 with an *empty body* (not `[]`; a naive `.json()` throws what looks like a bug), and bad parameters and rate-limit breaches return 200 with plain Chinese error strings. **Two registers, two enums** — 公司登記 and 商業登記, with separately downloadable tables of **33** and **9** codes; the distinctions that must survive are `01 核准設立` vs everything else, then `已清算完結` (entity gone) vs `解散`/`破產` (process running, estate may remain). **Search requires a status and matches substrings** — `Company_Status` is mandatory, so default to `01` and say so; `台積電` returns a lift company and a machinery firm, **not TSMC**.

**Two things Taiwan gives free.** `Register_Organization_Desc` arrives pre-populated (`臺中市政府`) — D-027(b)'s `register_office`, verbatim, no derivation. And the directors endpoint returns positions, names, corporate-nominee names and **shareholdings** — richer than UK PSC and brreg's `/roller`, squarely inside D-028's four preconditions, so `include=["officers"]` or nothing.

**Three decisions come out corroborated.** D-027 survives both countries (China's sub-national key is *inside* the identifier; Taiwan's arrives as a field). D-028(1) is the line between PIPL's usable and unusable bases, not merely a European preference. D-011's "no third value meaning both" is what HK's two-phase suppression demands.

## The three questions that matter most

1. **Does GB 32100-2015 say what everyone thinks it says?** The weights, the 31-character alphabet (I/O/S/V/Z removed) and MOD 31-3 come from the scheme's public description, not the standard; Amendment No. 1 is unread. This gates the whole `CN` module — twenty minutes with a browser, or via `cods.org.cn`, which is reachable from Norway.
2. **Is the GCIS source-IP whitelist enforced, or is the 告知書 a courtesy?** Twelve unregistered calls succeeded and the documented rejection never appeared. If enforced but lax, a hosted TW module could be switched off without notice.
3. **Would a Nordic or UK buyer route a Chinese-supplier check through an agent?** Not answerable here. Evidence is indirect: Qichacha names the job itself (*"供应链上下游信息核实"*, RMB 1.00/call); China Checkup sells English verification reports to foreign buyers (**price not found — the folder's most important commercial gap**); two MCP servers claimed the job and were abandoned the day they were created. None of the 2027 forcing functions reach here — Norwegian e-invoicing and AMLR Art. 23(4) are duties *about* counterparties and neither makes a Chinese excerpt obtainable. If the answer is no, **`TW` still ships on its own merits** and the rest of this folder is a note in the file.

**Also unverified:** the Data Security Law and 2023 Counter-Espionage revision (both npc.gov.cn URLs served the generic index — low confidence, re-source before use); every press citation for the 2023–25 access curbs; the gov.cn text of 市场主体登记管理条例 (2022) and 企业信息公示暂行条例 (2014, rev. 2024); any official SAMR status enum or NECIPS licence; Taiwan's actual rate limits (three controls documented, **zero numbers**); every Taiwanese filing deadline (ship `published_deadlines: []`); all prices except Qichacha's; whether HK permits redistribution of a purchased report; Glama/Smithery listings; everything about Macau.
